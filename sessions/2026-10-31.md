# Day 12 — Critical reasoning: find the gap between the evidence and the claim (AILET Logical Reasoning)

## Lesson

**Why this, today.** Critical reasoning is weight 3 in AILET, CLAT and NLSAT alike — the single most portable skill in
your plan. AILET asks it as short standalone arguments (3–5 lines, one question each), so speed comes from recognising
the question type instantly and knowing what the right answer must do.

### The anatomy — every argument has three parts
1. **Conclusion** — what the author wants you to accept. Signals: *therefore, so, thus, hence, should, must*. Test:
   ask "why?" of each sentence; the one the others answer is the conclusion.
2. **Premises** — the evidence offered. Signals: *because, since, data show, after all*.
3. **The gap (assumption)** — the unstated bridge from evidence to claim. Almost every CR question is really about
   this gap: *assumption* names it, *weaken* attacks it, *strengthen* fills it, *flaw* describes it.

### The method — 3 steps, ~60 seconds
1. Read the **question stem first** (it tells you what to hunt for).
2. Split the argument: conclusion | evidence. Say the gap in your own words before looking at options.
3. Pick the option that touches the gap. Discard options about the topic in general.

### The five gaps examiners love
- **Correlation → cause:** A and B occur together, so A caused B. Gap: no third factor, no reverse causation.
- **Measurement ≠ reality:** fewer *recorded* complaints → fewer *unhappy* customers. Gap: the measure tracks the thing.
- **Sample → all:** a few cases generalised.
- **Attack the person:** reject a proposal because of who proposed it.
- **Possible → necessary / necessary → sufficient.**

### Two tools for the hardest types
- **Negation test (assumption):** negate the option. If the argument collapses, it is the assumption.
- **Paradox:** the right answer explains how *both* facts are true; it does not deny either.

### Worked example (60 s)
*"Gyms that installed music systems saw members stay 20% longer per visit. So music makes people exercise more."*
**Q. Which most weakens?** (A) Music systems are costly. (B) The same gyms extended opening hours that month.
(C) Some members dislike loud music. (D) Members who exercise longer report higher satisfaction.
**Answer: (B)** — a third factor that explains longer visits without music. (A) is about cost, not the causal link;
(C) is "some", too weak to dent an average; (D) is about a different link.

**The trap examiners set.** The *true-but-irrelevant* option: a sensible statement on the same topic that never touches
the gap (like A above). On weaken/strengthen, ask of every option: *does this change how likely the conclusion is, given
the evidence?* If not, cut it — however reasonable it sounds.

## Test — 8 questions, AILET pattern (+1 / −0.25 wrong, 0 unattempted). Aim ≤ 9 minutes.

**1.** Municipal records show that wards which installed brighter LED streetlights in 2024 recorded fewer night-time
thefts in 2025 than wards that did not. Brighter lighting is also far cheaper than extra night patrols. Lighting should
therefore be the first item in any city's crime-prevention budget. Critics who call it cosmetic forget that thieves
prefer darkness.
Which of the following best states the main conclusion of the argument?
- (A) Brighter lighting reduced night-time thefts in the wards that installed it.
- (B) Brighter lighting is cheaper than extra night patrols.
- (C) Lighting should be the first item in a city's crime-prevention budget.
- (D) Thieves prefer darkness.

**2.** A company's recorded customer complaints fell by 30% in the six months after it replaced its email helpdesk
with a 24-hour chat helpline. The company concludes that the chat helpline has made its customers more satisfied.
The company's conclusion depends on which of the following assumptions?
- (A) The chat helpline costs less to run than the email helpdesk did.
- (B) Every customer of the company prefers chat to email.
- (C) Customer satisfaction cannot be measured accurately.
- (D) The fall in recorded complaints is not because grievances raised on the chat helpline are left out of the complaint count.

**3.** Refer to the argument in Question 1. Which of the following, if true, most weakens the claim that brighter
lighting caused the fall in night-time thefts?
- (A) In 2024 the wards that installed LED lights also opened new police outposts; the other wards did not.
- (B) LED streetlights consume less electricity than the lamps they replaced.
- (C) Some residents of the LED wards complained about glare.
- (D) Daytime thefts across the whole city rose slightly in 2025.

**4.** Students who take handwritten notes score higher on conceptual tests than students who type their notes on
laptops. A researcher concludes that handwriting, being slower, forces students to summarise in their own words instead
of transcribing word for word, and that this summarising deepens understanding.
Which of the following, if true, most strengthens the researcher's explanation?
- (A) Students who handwrite notes tend to have had higher grades before the study began.
- (B) Laptops are more expensive than notebooks.
- (C) When typists were instructed not to copy the lecturer word for word, the gap between the two groups narrowed sharply.
- (D) Handwritten notes are harder to share with classmates.

**5.** Ananya: "The councillor's proposal to cut the speed limit near schools should be rejected. After all, the
councillor was fined for speeding last year."
Which of the following best describes the flaw in Ananya's reasoning?
- (A) It assumes what it sets out to prove.
- (B) It rejects a proposal on the basis of a fact about the person making it rather than the merits of the proposal.
- (C) It generalises from a single, unrepresentative case to all speed limits.
- (D) It mistakes a correlation for a cause.

**6.** After a city made bus travel free for women, the number of fare-paying passengers fell sharply. Yet the bus
corporation's fare income in the following year was higher than before the scheme.
Which of the following, if true, best explains this apparent discrepancy?
- (A) The government pays the corporation the standard fare for every free journey taken by women.
- (B) Before the scheme, many women travelled by autorickshaw rather than bus.
- (C) Fares for men were left unchanged.
- (D) Some women continued to buy tickets voluntarily for a few weeks.

**7.** *(Review — Contract)* *Principle:* The communication of an acceptance is complete as against the proposer when it
is put in the course of transmission to him so as to be out of the acceptor's power, and as against the acceptor when it
comes to the knowledge of the proposer. The communication of a revocation is complete as against the person to whom it
is made when it comes to his knowledge. An acceptance may be revoked at any time before the communication of the
acceptance is complete as against the acceptor, but not afterwards.
*Facts:* On 3 November Tara posts a letter offering to sell her laptop to Dev for ₹50,000. Dev receives it and, at 10 a.m.
on 4 November, posts his acceptance. At 4 p.m. the same day he changes his mind and emails Tara withdrawing his
acceptance; she reads the email at 6 p.m. Dev's letter of acceptance reaches Tara on 6 November.
- (A) There is a contract, because it was concluded the moment Dev posted his acceptance, so his withdrawal came too late.
- (B) There is a contract, because an acceptance sent by letter cannot be revoked by email.
- (C) There is no contract, because Tara never agreed to Dev's withdrawal.
- (D) There is no contract, because Dev's withdrawal came to Tara's knowledge before his acceptance did.

**8.** *(Review — Principle application)* *Principle:* Nothing is an offence which is done by a person who, by reason of
a mistake of fact and not by reason of a mistake of law, in good faith believes himself to be justified by law in doing
it.
*Facts:* (Assume for this question that the law requires a government permit to fell a sandalwood tree, even on private
land.) Kiran knows that the tree on his farm is a sandalwood tree. A friend tells him, wrongly, that a landowner may cut
any tree on his own land. Believing this, Kiran fells the tree without a permit and is prosecuted.
- (A) Kiran has a defence, because he relied in good faith on what his friend told him.
- (B) Kiran has no defence, because his mistake was about what the law requires, not about any fact.
- (C) Kiran has a defence, because the tree stood on his own land.
- (D) Kiran has no defence, because he did not take care to find out what kind of tree it was.
