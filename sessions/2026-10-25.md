# Day 6 — Contract review, AILET speed: when "I accept" isn't acceptance

## Lesson

**Two-line recap of the clocks (ss. 4–5) — the part you missed on Day 2.**
- A **revocation** counts only when it **reaches** the other side.
- Proposer's deadline = acceptance **posted**. Acceptor's deadline = acceptance **reaching** the proposer.
  Compare the revocation's *arrival* with the right deadline; the posting date of a revocation decides nothing.

**New today: s. 7 — acceptance must be absolute and unqualified.**
1. **Counter-offer kills the offer.** "I accept, but at ₹4.5 lakh" is not acceptance; it is a new proposal *and*
   a rejection of the old one. If the counter is refused, the original offer cannot be revived by "fine, I'll
   take your original price" — that is now just a fresh offer the other side may refuse. (English root: *Hyde v Wrench*, 1840.)
2. **Inquiry ≠ counter-offer.** "Would you take payment in two instalments?" asks for information; the offer
   survives and can still be accepted. (*Stevenson v McLean*, 1880.)
3. **Prescribed manner (s. 7(2)).** If the proposal says how to accept and the acceptor uses another way, the
   proposer may insist on the prescribed manner — but **only within a reasonable time** after the acceptance
   reaches him. Stay silent too long and he has accepted the acceptance.
4. **Silence is not acceptance.** A proposer cannot say "if I don't hear from you, it's a deal". (*Felthouse v Bindley*, 1862.)
5. **Acceptance must come from the acceptor (or someone authorised).** News leaked by an unauthorised person
   is not communication of acceptance. (*Powell v Lee*, 1908.)

**Worked example (AILET style, 45 seconds).**
*Principle:* Acceptance must be absolute; a qualified reply is a counter-proposal that ends the original proposal.
*Facts:* Tanvi offers her bike to Uday for ₹60,000. Uday replies, "Accepted, if you fit new tyres." Tanvi says no.
Next day Uday writes, "OK, ₹60,000 without tyres." Tanvi has meanwhile sold to someone else.
*Answer:* No contract. "If you fit new tyres" added a term → counter-proposal → original offer dead. Uday's second
letter is a fresh offer Tanvi never accepted.

**The traps.**
- Words like "accepted" in a reply that adds a term — the label does not matter; the added term does.
- Treating a polite question as a counter-offer (it isn't).
- Forgetting the *time limit* on the proposer's right to insist on the prescribed mode.
- AILET: no penalty for unattempted. If you can't eliminate to two in ~50 s, skip.

## Test — 8 questions, aim for 7 minutes, +1 / −0.25 (unattempted 0)

Apply only the principle given in each question.

**1.** *Principle:* An acceptance may be revoked before it comes to the knowledge of the proposer, but not afterwards. A revocation is complete as against the proposer when it comes to his knowledge.
*Facts:* Isha posts her acceptance of Hari's offer on 5 March; it reaches Hari on 8 March. On 6 March she sends a courier letter revoking her acceptance; it reaches Hari on 9 March.
- (A) No contract, because Isha sent her revocation before her acceptance reached Hari.
- (B) No contract, because Isha revoked before Hari replied.
- (C) Contract, because the acceptance reached Hari before the revocation did.
- (D) Contract, because a posted acceptance can never be revoked.

**2.** *Principle:* A proposal may be revoked before the acceptance is put in course of transmission against the proposer, but not afterwards. A revocation is complete as against the acceptor when it comes to her knowledge.
*Facts:* Ravi sends a revocation of his offer to Sunita by speed post on 9 June; it reaches her on 11 June. Sunita had posted her acceptance on 10 June.
- (A) No contract, because Ravi's revocation was sent before Sunita's acceptance.
- (B) Contract, because Ravi's revocation did not reach Sunita before she posted her acceptance.
- (C) No contract, because Sunita's acceptance reached Ravi after he sent the revocation.
- (D) Contract, because an offer once posted can never be revoked.

**3.** *Principle:* An acceptance must be absolute and unqualified. A reply that varies the terms is a counter-proposal and operates as a rejection of the original proposal.
*Facts:* Vikram offers to sell his car to Wasim for ₹5 lakh. Wasim replies, "I accept at ₹4.5 lakh." Vikram refuses. Wasim then writes, "Fine, I accept your price of ₹5 lakh." Vikram sells the car to Yash.
- (A) Contract between Vikram and Wasim at ₹5 lakh, because Wasim finally accepted Vikram's own price.
- (B) Contract at ₹4.5 lakh, because Wasim accepted first.
- (C) No contract, because Wasim's counter-proposal ended Vikram's offer and his later letter was only a fresh offer.
- (D) No contract, because Vikram was free to sell to a higher bidder at any time.

**4.** *Principle:* same as Q3. A mere request for information does not vary the terms and is not a counter-proposal.
*Facts:* Zoya offers 200 tonnes of steel to Arjun at ₹48,000 a tonne. Arjun replies, "Would you accept payment in two instalments? If not, please say what is the longest credit you can give." Before Zoya answers, Arjun sends an unconditional acceptance of her original terms.
- (A) Contract, because Arjun's first reply was an inquiry, so the offer was still open when he accepted.
- (B) No contract, because asking about instalments is a counter-proposal.
- (C) No contract, because Zoya never replied to the inquiry.
- (D) Contract on instalment terms, because Zoya did not object to them.

**5.** *Principle:* If a proposal prescribes the manner of acceptance and the acceptance is made in another manner, the proposer may, within a reasonable time after it is communicated to him, insist on the prescribed manner; if he fails to do so, he accepts the acceptance.
*Facts:* Bhavna's written offer to Chetan says, "Accept only by registered post." Chetan accepts by e-mail, which Bhavna reads the same day. She says nothing for three weeks, then claims there is no contract because the e-mail was not registered post.
- (A) No contract, because the prescribed manner was not followed.
- (B) Contract, because Bhavna did not insist on the prescribed manner within a reasonable time.
- (C) No contract, because an e-mail cannot be a valid acceptance.
- (D) Contract, because the manner of acceptance never matters.

**6.** *Principle:* Acceptance must be communicated; a proposer cannot impose a contract by declaring that the other party's silence will amount to acceptance.
*Facts:* Dev writes to his cousin Esha, "I'll buy your horse for ₹1.2 lakh. If I hear nothing by Friday, I'll take it the horse is mine." Esha intends to sell and tells her stable-hand not to sell the horse, but never writes back. On Saturday the stable-hand sells it by mistake.
- (A) Contract between Dev and Esha, because Esha intended to accept and kept the horse aside.
- (B) Contract, because Dev fixed Friday as the deadline and nothing was heard.
- (C) No contract, because Esha never communicated acceptance to Dev.
- (D) No contract, because the stable-hand sold the horse.

**7.** *Principle:* Acceptance must be communicated to the proposer by the acceptor or by a person authorised by the acceptor.
*Facts:* A school committee interviews Farid for the post of headmaster and votes to appoint him, but resolves nothing about telling him. One committee member, acting on his own, phones Farid that night: "You've got it." Next week the committee rescinds its vote and appoints someone else.
- (A) Contract, because the committee had voted to appoint Farid.
- (B) Contract, because Farid came to know of the decision.
- (C) No contract, because the committee could change its mind at any time before Farid joined.
- (D) No contract, because the acceptance was communicated by a person not authorised to do so.

**8.** *Principle:* A proposal is revoked by the death of the proposer if that fact comes to the knowledge of the acceptor before acceptance.
*Facts:* Naveen posts an offer to sell his antique clock to Pooja on 1 May and dies on 3 May. Pooja posts her acceptance on 4 May. Which additional fact, if true, means **there is no contract**?
- (A) Naveen's son did not want the clock sold.
- (B) Pooja's acceptance reached Naveen's house only on 6 May.
- (C) Pooja learnt of Naveen's death on 7 May.
- (D) On the morning of 4 May, before posting her acceptance, Pooja read a news report of Naveen's death.
