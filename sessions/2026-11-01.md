# Day 13 — Current affairs, CLAT style: read the passage, answer from what you know

## Lesson

**Why this, today.** CLAT's Current Affairs section is roughly a quarter of the paper (about 28–32 questions) and you
have zero data on it. It is also the most "learnable" section in five weeks: it rewards a system, not talent.

### How CLAT asks current affairs
CLAT gives a ~450-word passage on a recent event, often with a word or name **redacted** ("[1]"). The questions then
ask about facts *around* the event — the venue, the body in charge, the earlier editions, the scheme behind it. The
passage is a hook, **not** the answer bank: typically only one or two answers sit in the text.

### Method — the "five-ring" note for every big event
When you read news, write one line per ring:
1. **What & when** — exact name, dates, edition number.
2. **Where** — city and venue.
3. **Who** — host ministry/body, chair, key participants.
4. **Lineage** — previous editions (host + year) and the next host.
5. **Output** — the named declaration/report, its pillars/themes, numbers (signatories, money).
Examiners build four options from these rings, so one note per event covers ~5 questions.

### Worked example
*Passage extract:* "…the third summit in the series, held in [1] in February 2025, was renamed the AI Action Summit…"
**Q. Which city is [1]?** (A) London (B) Seoul (C) Paris (D) Geneva. **Answer: (C) Paris.** The lineage ring:
Bletchley Park, UK (Nov 2023, AI Safety Summit) → Seoul, South Korea (May 2024) → Paris, France (Feb 2025, AI Action
Summit) → New Delhi, India (Feb 2026, AI Impact Summit) → Geneva, Switzerland (2027). London and Seoul are real
earlier rings; Geneva is the *next* one — that is how distractors are made.

### The trap examiners set
**Neighbouring facts.** Options are rarely random: they are the right answer's neighbours — the previous host instead of
the next, a sister scheme's outlay, the old venue instead of the new one. Learn each ring precisely, and when two options
both feel familiar, ask "which ring is this question on?".

## Test — 8 questions, CLAT pattern (+1 / −0.25 wrong, 0 unattempted). Aim ≤ 8 minutes.

**Passage (Questions 1–6)**

In February 2026, New Delhi hosted the India AI Impact Summit, the first summit in the global series on artificial
intelligence to be held in a country of the Global South. The main sessions met at [1], the convention centre that had
earlier served as the venue of India's G20 leaders' summit. The meeting was the fourth in a sequence that began with a
safety-focused gathering in the United Kingdom in 2023 and continued in [2] in 2024 and in Paris in 2025. Each host has
renamed the summit to signal its own priorities: "safety" became "action", and India chose "impact", arguing that the
real test of AI is whether its benefits reach ordinary people rather than only the firms and states that build it.

India organised the deliberations around three "sutras" — [3] — and seven working tracks it called "chakras", ranging
from human capital and trustworthy AI to energy-efficient systems and the use of AI in science. The summit closed with
the New Delhi Declaration on AI Impact, endorsed by 88 countries and international organisations. Like the statements of
earlier summits, the declaration is not a treaty: it creates no binding obligations, and critics noted that voluntary
pledges have so far done little to narrow the gap between the few countries that own advanced computing capacity and the
many that do not. Supporters replied that consensus documents shape the agenda that binding rules later follow.

For India, the summit was also a showcase for domestic policy. The nodal ministry, [4], pointed to the IndiaAI Mission,
approved by the Union Cabinet in 2024, which funds shared computing capacity, datasets and start-ups. With the next
summit already fixed for [5], the question for New Delhi is whether "impact" will outlast the change of host.

**1.** Which venue has been redacted as [1]?
- (A) Yashobhoomi (India International Convention and Expo Centre), Dwarka
- (B) Bharat Mandapam, Pragati Maidan
- (C) Vigyan Bhawan
- (D) Sushma Swaraj Bhawan

**2.** Which city has been redacted as [2]?
- (A) Bletchley Park
- (B) Tokyo
- (C) Seoul
- (D) Geneva

**3.** Which of the following correctly fills [3], the three "sutras" of the summit?
- (A) People, Planet and Progress
- (B) Safety, Security and Sovereignty
- (C) Inclusion, Innovation and Infrastructure
- (D) Data, Democracy and Development

**4.** The next summit in the series, redacted as [5], is to be hosted by which country?
- (A) Japan
- (B) United Arab Emirates
- (C) Brazil
- (D) Switzerland

**5.** Which ministry has been redacted as [4]?
- (A) Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology
- (B) Ministry of Science and Technology
- (C) Ministry of External Affairs
- (D) Ministry of Communications

**6.** The IndiaAI Mission mentioned in the passage was approved with a total outlay of approximately:
- (A) ₹76,000 crore
- (B) ₹1 lakh crore
- (C) ₹2,000 crore
- (D) ₹10,372 crore

**7.** *(Review — Contract)* *Principle:* The communication of an acceptance is complete as against the proposer when it
is put in the course of transmission to him so as to be out of the power of the acceptor. The communication of a
revocation is complete as against the person to whom it is made when it comes to his knowledge. A proposal may be
revoked at any time before the communication of its acceptance is complete as against the proposer, but not afterwards.
*Facts:* On 2 November Meera posts a letter offering to sell her scooter to Arjun for ₹40,000. Arjun receives it on 4
November. At 10 a.m. on 5 November Meera emails Arjun withdrawing the offer. At 11 a.m. Arjun, who has not checked his
email, posts his letter of acceptance. He reads Meera's email at 3 p.m. Arjun's letter reaches Meera on 7 November.
- (A) There is no contract, because Meera sent her withdrawal before Arjun posted his acceptance.
- (B) There is no contract, because Meera did not receive the acceptance until 7 November.
- (C) There is a contract, because the acceptance was complete as against Meera at 11 a.m., before her withdrawal came to
  Arjun's knowledge.
- (D) There is a contract, because an offer made by post can be withdrawn only by post.

**8.** *(Review — Principle application)* *Principle:* Nothing is an offence which is done by a person who, by reason of
a mistake of fact and not by reason of a mistake of law, in good faith believes himself to be justified by law in doing
it.
*Facts:* The law authorises a railway ticket examiner to detain, until the next station, a passenger travelling in a
reserved coach without a valid ticket. Sunita, a ticket examiner, checks the official reservation chart, which — owing to
a software error she has no means of detecting — shows seat 42 as unbooked. Rohan, sitting in seat 42, holds a valid
ticket booked an hour earlier but cannot show it because his phone battery is dead. Sunita detains him and is later
charged with wrongful confinement.
- (A) Sunita has no defence, because Rohan in fact held a valid ticket.
- (B) Sunita has a defence, because she acted in good faith on a mistaken belief about a fact — whether seat 42 was
  booked.
- (C) Sunita has a defence, because a public servant can never be prosecuted for acts done on duty.
- (D) Sunita has no defence, because her mistake was one of law.
